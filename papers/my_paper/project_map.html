A wrapper that upgrades graph fraud detectors

Everything you need to talk through the work: the problem, the system, the pipeline, the code, what was actually run, what it produced, where it stops — and the questions you will be asked.

01

The thirty-second version

"Fraudsters hide by connecting to honest accounts. I built three defences into one system, and measured what each one is actually worth."

If you say one thing It is not a competitor to the best detector on the benchmark. It is an upgrade path for detectors that filter neighbours — and the unusual part is that every component was measured separately, including the two that turned out not to work.

"A graph network decides what you are by looking at your neighbours. So fraudsters buy honest neighbours."

The finding people remember The design nearly reported its own working component as a failure. Under the original coupling the adaptive pillar measured +0.19 pp, p = 0.10 — a null. Removing the coupling revealed +0.58 pp on 20 of 20 seeds. Hand-designed connections between components can suppress the very thing they were meant to assist, and only a de-confounded ablation finds it.
08

Where it stops

"Say these before anyone asks. It is much stronger coming from you."

BoundaryThe honest statement
scope It cannot wrap every model. The policy needs a neighbour-selection step and gradients, so tree ensembles and spectral GNNs are out. The claim is "selection-based GNN detectors."
evidence All twenty seeds share one data split. The p-values measure reproducibility across restarts, not across datasets. Multi-split runs are in progress.
null Combining the pillars adds nothing beyond their sum. +0.58 and +1.96 give +2.70 — an interaction of +0.16 pp against 0.43 pp of noise. They are additive, not synergistic.
null The language model is not necessary. A free scorer matches it on every graph backbone. The value is in which six quantities are measured, not who measures them.
metric Ranking improves; the fixed-threshold F₁ does not. Flat on YelpChi, negative on Amazon. Likely a calibration shift — being tested.
host 2 On the second host nothing reached significance at five seeds. The variance there is 20× larger; a twenty-seed run is queued to settle it.
09

The questions you will get

Drawn from a five-reviewer panel that read the paper cold. These are the real ones.

"XGBoost gets 0.9177 in your own table. Why would anyone use this?"
Because it is not a rival to XGBoost — it is a wrapper for detectors that filter neighbours, and XGBoost has no neighbour-filtering step to attach to. The comparison that matters is base versus base-plus-wrapper. And note the six scores improve XGB-Graph too: that is the same contribution reaching a host the policy cannot. base → +wrapper is the claim · 0.9106 → 0.9177 on XGB-Graph with the scores alone
"If a free heuristic matches the LLM, why is this an LLM paper?"
It should not be, and the revision says so. The contribution is which six quantities to measure; the language model is one convenient way to compute them, and on graph backbones a zero-cost scorer is statistically indistinguishable. That is a result worth having — the 2025 wave of LLM-enhanced detectors assumes the model is load-bearing and none of them ran this control. n.s. on all four graph backbones · significant only on feature-only classifiers
"Twenty seeds, but one split. What do your p-values actually mean?"
They mean "this reproduces across restarts," not "this generalises to other data." That is a real limitation and I state it rather than hide it. Multi-split runs are under way; the Amazon replication on a different dataset already gives independent evidence the effect is not split-specific. train.py hardcodes random_state=2 · Amazon +0.51 pp, 19/20 seeds
"Half a percentage point. Does that matter operationally?"
Not demonstrated yet, and I will not claim it. The gain is in ranking quality; F₁ at a fixed threshold does not move. Precision-at-k and recall-at-fixed-FPR are the right measures and they are computable from predictions already saved. What is established is variance reduction — on Amazon the policy halves seed-to-seed spread, which matters for anyone retraining on a schedule. ±0.0027 → ±0.0011 seed std on Amazon
"What is actually novel here?"
Three things, none of them "we combined three techniques." First, a portable six-dimensional text signal that improves seven of eight model families. Second, the first systematic test of whether the LLM in LLM-enhanced graph fraud detection is necessary — it is not. Third, a de-confounded ablation that found a coupling in my own design suppressing the component it was built to help. the combination is new; what it buys is +0.16 pp, which I report as a null
"You call it a three-pillar framework, but the pillars do not interact."
Correct, and that is a finding rather than a defence. I built both intended cross-pillar interfaces and measured them: one contributes nothing, the other actively hurt. The pillars are additive. Anyone planning to integrate an LLM into an RL filtering policy should know that before spending the effort. scores-in-state −0.33 pp p=0.44 · prior initialisation −0.71 pp
"Your labels come from Yelp's own filter. Are you detecting fraud or imitating a filter?"
A fair challenge and one I cannot fully rule out. If that filter keys on linguistic style, the text scores may partly be recovering its heuristics — which would also explain why a crude scorer matches a language model. Discriminating between the two needs human-adjudicated labels or cross-platform transfer. I state it as an open alternative rather than assuming the flattering reading. YelpChi labels originate from Rayana & Akoglu, KDD 2015
