Online review platforms host coordinated fraud operations in which malicious users post fake reviews and manipulate ratings, distorting consumer trust and inflicting measurable economic harm. Because fraud is fundamentally a relational phenomenon---fraudsters act in groups, share targets, and recycle behaviour patterns---graph neural networks (GNNs) have become a natural detection substrate, modelling users and their interactions as multi-relation graphs over which discriminative representations can be learned~\cite{wu2021graph}.

Sophisticated fraudsters, however, deliberately evade neighbourhood aggregation. They manipulate their attributes to resemble legitimate users (\emph{feature camouflage}) and form connections to benign entities to dilute the suspiciousness of their neighbourhoods (\emph{relation camouflage})~\cite{dou2020enhancing}. Camouflage breaks the core inductive bias of message-passing GNNs: aggregated neighbour information no longer correlates reliably with the node label. Defending against camouflage requires three complementary capabilities operating in concert:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Structural reasoning} --- multi-relation aggregation that exposes the relational signatures camouflage tries to hide.
    \item \textbf{Adaptive reasoning} --- per-node, per-relation control over which neighbours to trust, since the right level of aggressiveness varies across the graph.
    \item \textbf{Semantic reasoning} --- access to fraud signals beyond the behavioural feature space, particularly textual cues that camouflaged graph structure cannot suppress.
\end{itemize}

Existing work addresses these capabilities only in isolation. CARE-GNN~\cite{dou2020enhancing} introduced reinforcement-learning-guided neighbour selection, but uses a heuristic Bernoulli bandit that maintains a single global filtering threshold per relation, updated by a fixed step-size rule; its filtering decisions cannot adapt per-node and cannot incorporate any signal beyond a single label-aware distance metric. RioGNN~\cite{peng2022reinforced} refined the RL approach with recursive multi-relation policies but retained the structural-only state representation. Recent integrations of large language models with GNNs for fraud detection---most notably FLAG~\cite{yang2025flag}---augment node features and sampling decisions with LLM-derived semantic signals, but lack a learned policy for dynamic relation weighting and treat the LLM as a feature encoder rather than a reasoning component routed through both structure and policy. No prior work unifies structural, adaptive, and semantic reasoning within a single end-to-end framework.

We argue that this unification is not incidental but necessary. Structural reasoning alone fails under camouflage. Adaptive reasoning alone, without external signal, can only refine what the structural pillar already sees. Semantic reasoning alone, without structural and adaptive grounding, treats fraud as a text classification problem and ignores the relational structure that defines coordinated fraud. The three modes address complementary failure modes, and we show that a framework integrating all three produces capabilities none of the pairs can match.

This paper proposes such a framework. Concretely, we introduce a three-pillar architecture in which CARE-GNN~\cite{dou2020enhancing} serves as the \emph{structural} pillar, providing the multi-relation backbone for camouflage-resistant aggregation. The \emph{adaptive} pillar replaces CARE-GNN's heuristic bandit with the Camouflage-Aware Policy Network (CAPN)---an actor--critic policy that produces per-node, per-relation filtering thresholds conditioned on a rich state vector capturing structural, label, and semantic signals. The \emph{semantic} pillar uses a large language model both to compute structural-reasoning scores that enrich the policy state and to score fraud-specific textual risk signals, which are injected into node features through a gated residual module preserving feature dimensionality. All language-model outputs are precomputed once and cached, so training cost is independent of the external model. The three pillars are trained end-to-end under a single joint objective.

\paragraph{Contributions.} The contributions of this paper are as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We propose the first end-to-end framework that unifies multi-relation graph reasoning, learned reinforcement-learning-based neighbour filtering, and LLM-derived semantic signals for camouflage-resistant fraud detection on review graphs.
    \item We introduce the Camouflage-Aware Policy Network (CAPN), an actor--critic policy that replaces CARE-GNN's heuristic bandit with per-node, per-relation thresholds conditioned on a state vector that fuses structural, label, and LLM-derived signals.
    \item We design a gated residual injection mechanism for textual risk scores that preserves feature dimensionality and lets the network selectively unlearn harmful text dimensions, avoiding the degradation observed under naive concatenation.
    \item We empirically validate the framework on the Amazon and YelpChi benchmarks, showing that the unified three-pillar model outperforms CARE-GNN and recent baselines, and we provide component-level ablations that attribute the gains to specific design decisions.
\end{enumerate}
